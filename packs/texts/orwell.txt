# title: Why I Write (excerpt)
# source: George Orwell, "Why I Write", 1946
# license: public domain in most jurisdictions

From a very early age, perhaps the age of five or six, I knew that when I grew up I should be a writer. Between the ages of about seventeen and twenty-four I tried to abandon this idea, but I did so with the consciousness that I was outraging my true nature and that sooner or later I should have to settle down and write books.

I had the lonely child's habit of making up stories and holding conversations with imaginary persons, and I think from the very start my literary ambitions were mixed up with the feeling of being isolated and undervalued. I knew that I had a facility with words and a power of facing unpleasant facts, and I felt that this created a sort of private world in which I could get my own back for my failure in everyday life.

---

从很小的时候，也许五六岁，我就知道长大后要当作家。十七岁到二十四岁之间，我试过放弃这个念头，但我知道那是在违背自己的天性，迟早要安定下来写书。

我有个孤独孩子的习惯：编故事，和想象中的人对话。我想从一开始，我的文学野心就和被孤立、被轻视的感觉混在一起。我知道自己有驾驭文字的能力，也有面对不快事实的勇气，我觉得这造出了一个私人世界，让我能在其中为日常生活的失败扳回一局。
